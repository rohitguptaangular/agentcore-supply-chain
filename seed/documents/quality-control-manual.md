# Quality Control Manual

Document reference: QA-QCM-001
Revision: 11
Owner: Quality Assurance

These procedures are fictional and exist only to give the knowledge base
realistic material to retrieve.

## 1. Inspection triggers

An inspection is raised when any of the following occurs:

- Receipt of a batch from a supplier whose last inspection result was not a
  clear pass
- Receipt of any batch of a product in the Hydraulics category
- A customer complaint referencing a specific batch
- Routine sampling per the schedule in section 3

## 2. Inspection results

Three outcomes are recorded:

- **PASS** — the batch meets the applicable standard and is released to stock
- **CONDITIONAL_PASS** — the batch is within tolerance but trending toward the
  limit; it is released and the next batch is inspected at double sample size
- **FAIL** — the batch is rejected and remains in quarantine

A conditional pass is not a failure, but three consecutive conditional passes
for the same product are treated as a failure for supplier scoring purposes.

## 3. Sampling

Sample sizes follow the acceptable quality limit recorded in the standards
register for the product category. The sampling plan for each category is
authoritative; inspectors may increase a sample size but never reduce it.

Where a batch exceeds one thousand units, the sample size is increased by fifty
percent over the standard plan.

## 4. Handling a failed batch

1. Leave the batch in the quarantine location. Do not return it to general
   stock under any circumstances.
2. Record the inspection with the defect count, sample size and a description
   of the defect mode.
3. Raise a corrective action against the supplier within two working days.
4. Notify procurement so that replenishment can be re-sourced if required.
5. Set the affected product to under review in the compliance register until
   the corrective action is closed.

## 5. Corrective actions

A corrective action requires the supplier to provide a root cause analysis
within ten working days and a containment plan within two.

The corrective action is closed only after two subsequent batches pass
inspection at the standard sample size.

## 6. Calibration

All measuring equipment used for inspection is calibrated every six months
against traceable reference standards. Equipment outside its calibration window
may not be used, and any inspection performed with out-of-calibration equipment
is void and must be repeated.

## 7. Records and retention

Inspection records are retained for seven years. Each record carries the
inspection identifier, product identifier, batch identifier, date, result,
defect count, sample size and inspector.

Records supporting a customer claim are retained until the claim is closed plus
a further seven years.

## 8. Escalation

A failed batch of a product with an open customer order is escalated to the
quality manager and the supply chain manager on the day the result is recorded.

Two failures of the same product within a rolling ninety day period trigger a
supplier audit regardless of the scheduled audit date.
