# Inventory Management Procedures

Document reference: OPS-IMP-004
Revision: 7
Owner: Warehouse Operations

These procedures are fictional and exist only to give the knowledge base
realistic material to retrieve.

## 1. Scope

This document covers the receipt, storage, counting and replenishment of goods
across all distribution centres. It does not cover finished-goods dispatch,
which is described in the Logistics Handbook.

## 2. Definitions

- **Reorder point** — the on-hand quantity at which replenishment must be
  raised for a product.
- **Cycle count** — a partial stock count performed on a rolling schedule
  rather than a full annual inventory.
- **Quarantine location** — a physically separated area holding stock that has
  not yet passed inspection.

## 3. Storage and location discipline

Every product has a single designated primary location, recorded in the
inventory system as the warehouse location code, for example WH-A-12. Stock
must not be stored outside its designated location without a movement record.

Aisle A holds fast-moving electrical and mechanical items. Aisle B holds
fasteners and bulk consumables. Aisle C holds hydraulics and any item requiring
temperature control between 5 and 25 degrees Celsius.

## 4. Cycle counting

Cycle counts run weekly. Each week covers one quarter of all active SKUs, so
every product is counted at least monthly.

A variance of more than two percent between counted and recorded quantity must
be escalated to the warehouse supervisor the same day. Variances above five
percent trigger a full recount of the affected location before any adjustment
is posted.

## 5. Goods inwards process

This section is also referred to as the receiving procedure.

### 5.1 Preparation

Confirm the receiving bay is clear and that pallet handling equipment is
available. Retrieve the expected purchase order from the system before the
carrier arrives.

### 5.2 Documentation check

On arrival, verify the carrier holds the correct shipment documentation: a
packing slip and, for international consignments, a bill of lading. Match each
line on the packing slip against the corresponding purchase order line. Do not
begin unloading if the documentation does not match the purchase order.

### 5.3 Physical receipt and inspection

Unload and count each line. Record the counted quantity against the purchase
order line, not the packing slip quantity, so that discrepancies are captured.

Inspect a sample of each line for transit damage. Any item showing damage is
moved to the quarantine location and recorded as rejected on receipt. Damage
must be photographed before the carrier leaves the site, as claims cannot be
raised afterwards.

Goods requiring quality inspection are receipted into quarantine and remain
there until an inspection result is recorded. See the Quality Control Manual
for sampling requirements by product category.

### 5.4 System entry

Enter received quantities into the inventory system within two hours of
unloading. Record discrepancies and damage against the receipt, including the
quarantine quantity where applicable.

### 5.5 Put-away

Move accepted stock to its designated location. Confirm the put-away in the
system so that the location record stays accurate.

### 5.6 Final verification

Check that the receipt total matches the sum of accepted, quarantined and
rejected quantities. Sign off the receiving documentation and forward a copy to
accounts payable and, where an inspection was required, to quality control.

## 6. Replenishment and reordering

When on-hand quantity falls to or below the reorder point, a replenishment
requisition must be raised within one working day.

Replenishment quantity is normally the reorder point multiplied by three,
adjusted for the supplier lead time recorded against the preferred supplier.
Products with a lead time above twenty days carry an additional two weeks of
buffer stock.

Discount codes may be applied at the point of raising the order. All discounts
and promotional pricing require prior approval from the procurement team; see
the Promotional Pricing Policy for the approval route and current code formats.

## 7. Escalation

Stock-outs of any product supporting an active customer contract are escalated
immediately to the supply chain manager. Persistent shortfalls of the same
product in three consecutive months trigger a supplier performance review.
